> Summary for guidance only, based on the ERC 2025 guidelines. Hands-on training is essential. Infant: under 1 year old. If they look like an adolescent or an adult, use the "Adult CPR" protocol.

## When
The child is **unresponsive** and **not breathing normally**. If in doubt, act as if they were not breathing.

## Steps
1. Check that the area is safe. Speak to them loudly and touch them; don't use painful stimuli.
2. Call **112** on **speaker** (or have someone else call) and follow the operator's instructions. If you are alone and have no phone, do **1 minute of CPR** before going for help.
3. Open the airway: one hand on the forehead and two fingers under the chin. In infants, head in a **neutral position** (slightly tilted); in older children, tilted a little more.
4. Give **5 initial rescue breaths**, 1 second each, just enough to make the chest rise. In infants, cover **their mouth and nose** with your mouth.
5. Compress the **lower half of the breastbone** by at least **one third of the depth of the chest** (about 4 cm in infants and 5 cm in children; never more than 6 cm), at **100–120 compressions per minute**. Let the chest rise fully.
6. Technique: in infants, **encircle the chest with both hands and compress with both thumbs**; in children, with **one or two hands** depending on their size.
7. Continue with **30 compressions and 2 rescue breaths** (15:2 only if you have specific training in paediatric CPR). If you cannot or do not want to give breaths, do compressions only.
8. Have someone else bring and attach an **AED** as soon as possible ("Using a defibrillator (AED)" protocol). Don't stop until professional help arrives or the child shows clear signs of life.

## Warnings
- If the chest does not rise when you give breaths: reposition the head and remove only what you can see in the mouth. **Don't** do blind finger sweeps.
- If they are unresponsive but breathing normally and there is no trauma: recovery position, and check their breathing at least every minute.
