> Summary for guidance only, based on the ERC 2025 guidelines. An AED is safe: it only delivers a shock if it detects a rhythm that needs one. Hands-on training is strongly recommended.

## When
The person is **unresponsive and not breathing normally**. Use it together with CPR.

## Steps
1. Have someone call **112** and another person bring the nearest **AED** (112 can tell you where there is one). **Don't interrupt CPR** while it is on its way.
2. **Switch it on** (open the lid or press the button) and follow its **voice prompts**.
3. Bare the chest; if it is wet, **dry it**. Move the bra aside if it gets in the way.
4. Attach the pads: one **below the right collarbone**, next to the breastbone, and the other on the **left side, below the armpit**. Don't place them over breast tissue.
5. Children under **25 kg** (about 8 years old): switch on **paediatric mode** if there is one and place one pad in the centre of the chest, slightly to the left, and the other on the back, **between the shoulder blades**. If there is no paediatric mode, use it in adult mode.
6. While it analyses the rhythm: **nobody must touch** the casualty.
7. If it advises a shock: check that nobody is touching them and **press the button** (some models deliver the shock automatically).
8. After the shock, or if no shock is advised, **resume CPR immediately** and follow the prompts. Don't remove the pads or switch the AED off.

## Warnings
- Every minute of delay in defibrillation reduces the chance of survival by around 10 %: don't wait until you are sure before using it.
- After drowning: get the casualty out of the water and dry the chest before attaching the pads.
