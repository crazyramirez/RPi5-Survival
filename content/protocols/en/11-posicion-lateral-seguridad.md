> Summary for guidance only, based on the ERC guidelines. It keeps the airway open in someone who is unresponsive but breathing. Hands-on training is strongly recommended.

## When
The person is **unresponsive** but **breathing normally**, and there is **no** suspicion of trauma (a fall, an accident).

## Steps
1. Call **112**.
2. Kneel beside them, with the casualty on their back and legs straight. Remove their glasses and any bulky objects from their pockets.
3. Place the **arm nearest** to you at a right angle to the body (straight or bent).
4. Bring the **other arm** across the chest and hold the back of their hand against their **cheek** nearest to you. Hold it there.
5. With your other hand, **bend the far knee**, with the foot flat on the ground.
6. **Pull that knee towards you** to roll them onto their side, keeping hold of the hand against the cheek.
7. Adjust the upper leg so that the hip and knee are at right angles.
8. **Tilt the head back** to keep the airway open, with the hand under the cheek.
9. **Check their breathing** continuously. If they stop breathing normally, turn them onto their back and start **CPR**.

## Warnings
- **Don't** use it if they are breathing abnormally (gasping) or if there is trauma: in that case don't move them unless there is danger.
- If they must stay like this for more than 30 minutes, turn them onto the other side to relieve pressure on the lower arm.
