> Summary for guidance only, based on the ERC 2025 guidelines. Hands-on training is essential. Infant: under 1 year old.

## If they can cough, cry or speak
- Encourage them to **keep coughing**. Watch them and don't hit them.

## If they cannot cough, cry or breathe properly
1. Have someone call **112** (or call yourself on speaker).
2. **Infant**: place them **face down along your forearm**, resting on your thigh, supporting the head with your hand and with the head **lower than the chest**. Give up to **5 back blows**, firmly, between the shoulder blades.
3. If that doesn't clear it: turn them **face up** on your thighs, encircle their chest with your hands and give up to **5 chest thrusts** with **both thumbs** on the breastbone, sharper than in CPR.
4. **Child (over 1 year old)**: lean them forward and give up to **5 back blows** between the shoulder blades.
5. If that doesn't clear it: stand behind them, put your arms around them, lean them forward, place your **fist between the navel and the bottom of the breastbone** and pull sharply **inwards and upwards**, up to **5 times**.
6. Alternate 5 blows and 5 thrusts while they remain conscious. **Stop** as soon as they cough, cry or breathe.
7. If they **become unresponsive**: call 112 if nobody has done so and start **CPR** ("CPR in children and infants" protocol).

## Warnings
- **Don't** do blind finger sweeps in the mouth; remove only an object that you can see clearly and grasp easily.
- **Never** give abdominal thrusts to an infant.
- After abdominal or chest thrusts, they must be assessed by a doctor even if they recover.
