> Summary for guidance only, based on the ERC 2025 guidelines. Don't try to diagnose: if in doubt, treat it as an emergency. First-aid training is recommended.

## Warning signs
Pain or **pressure in the chest** that may spread to the neck, jaw, back or left arm, often with cold sweat, shortness of breath, nausea or dizziness. Sometimes the symptoms are less clear.

## Steps
1. Call **112** immediately.
2. Have them **stop any exertion**: sit them down or lay them down in whatever position is most comfortable for them. Reassure them.
3. If they are a conscious adult with pain that seems to be of **cardiac origin**, they are **not allergic to aspirin** and 112 does not advise otherwise, encourage and help them to **chew** an aspirin (150–300 mg) themselves as soon as possible while waiting for the ambulance.
4. If they have **diagnosed angina**, help them take their own **nitroglycerin** (spray or tablet) as prescribed.
5. Loosen tight clothing and **don't leave them alone**.
6. Locate a nearby **AED** in case it is needed.
7. If they become unresponsive and are not breathing normally: **CPR** and AED.

## Warnings
- **Don't** let them drive, and don't take them yourself if you can wait for the ambulance.
- Tell 112 about their usual medication (for example, anticoagulants) before giving them aspirin.
