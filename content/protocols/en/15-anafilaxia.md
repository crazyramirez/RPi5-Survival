> Guidance summary of the ERC 2025 guidelines. Anaphylaxis can kill within minutes: adrenaline (epinephrine) is the first-choice treatment. Learn to use the auto-injector before you need it.

## Signs
After a food, a sting or a medicine: difficulty breathing, **wheezing** or hoarseness, **swelling of the lips, tongue or throat**, hives or redness, dizziness or fainting, cold and clammy skin, vomiting or abdominal pain.

## Steps
1. Call **112** and say "**anaphylaxis**".
2. If they have a **prescribed adrenaline auto-injector**, use it **immediately** on the **outer side of the thigh** following the device's instructions (it can be given through clothing). If what they have been prescribed is **nasal** adrenaline, use it.
3. Position: if they are struggling to breathe, **sitting up**; if they are dizzy or pale, **lying down** with legs raised. **Do not stand them up** or make them walk.
4. If symptoms **have not improved after 5 minutes**, give a **second dose** if there is another auto-injector.
5. Remove the trigger if possible (sting, stop eating).
6. Note the time of each dose and do not leave them alone: symptoms can **come back** hours later.
7. If they stop responding and are not breathing normally: **CPR**.

## Warnings
- **Do not** wait to see if they improve before using the adrenaline: if in doubt, use it.
- Antihistamines and inhalers **do not replace** adrenaline.
- Even if they improve, they must go to hospital.
