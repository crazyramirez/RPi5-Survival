> Guidance summary of the ERC 2025 guidelines. If in doubt, treat the casualty as if they have a spinal injury. Hands-on training is highly recommended.

## When to suspect it
A fall or jump from a height, **diving** into shallow water, a road traffic or sports accident, crushing by machinery or a heavy object. Neck or back pain, tingling or weakness in the arms or legs.

## Steps
1. Call **112**.
2. **Do not move them** unless there is imminent danger (fire, traffic, water).
3. If they are awake: ask them **not to move their neck** and to keep it in a comfortable, stable position. **Never** force them into a position.
4. If they do not respond and are lying on their back: kneel behind their head and **hold it with both hands**, in line with the body.
5. If you need to open the airway, push the jaw forward (**jaw thrust**) moving the neck as little as possible.
6. If they are face down and breathing, keep their neck stable. If they have to be turned over to open the airway or perform CPR, do it **as a single unit (log roll)** with several people, keeping head, neck and trunk aligned.
7. If they are not breathing normally: **CPR**. Resuscitation takes priority over protecting the spine.
8. Keep them warm and watch them until help arrives.

## Warnings
- **Do not** apply improvised neck collars.
- Do not sit them up or lift their head to give them a drink.
