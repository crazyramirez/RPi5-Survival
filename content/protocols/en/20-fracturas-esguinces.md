> Guidance summary. Without an X-ray a sprain cannot be reliably told apart from a fracture: if in doubt, treat it as a fracture.

## Signs of a possible fracture
Severe pain, deformity, swelling, inability to move or bear weight, a crack heard at the time of injury.

## Steps
1. If there is severe bleeding, control it first ("Severe bleeding" protocol).
2. **Do not move** the injured area. **Do not try to reset** the bone or straighten a deformed fracture.
3. **Immobilise** it in the position it is in, including the joint above and the joint below: with cardboard, rolled-up magazines, splints or the other leg, padding with clothing. For the arm, a **sling** made from a scarf.
4. Remove rings, watches and bracelets before it swells.
5. Check that the fingers or toes are still **warm and a good colour**; if not, loosen the support.
6. **Open fracture** (bone visible or a wound over the fracture): cover the wound with a clean dressing without pressing on the bone.
7. Call **112** if there is deformity, an open fracture, a suspected injury to the hip, thigh, pelvis or spine, or if the limb is cold, pale or numb.

## Mild sprains
- **Rest** and **elevation** of the area.
- **Cold** wrapped in a cloth (never ice directly on the skin) for up to 20 minutes, every 2–3 hours, for the first few days.
- If they cannot bear weight or it does not improve in a few days, have a health professional assess it.

## Warnings
- Do not apply heat or massage in the first few days.
