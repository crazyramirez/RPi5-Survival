> Guidance summary of the ERC 2025 guidelines.

## When to suspect it
A person with **diabetes** (or who is malnourished) who suddenly becomes sweaty, shaky, pale, confused, irritable, behaves strangely or is drowsy.

## Steps
1. If they are **awake and able to swallow**: give them **15–20 g of glucose** (glucose tablets or gel).
2. If there is no glucose: a handful of **sugary sweets** or **50–100 ml** of fruit juice or a sugary soft drink (not "light" or "zero").
3. If there is a glucose meter: measure their sugar. Below **70 mg/dl (4 mmol/l)**, treat it and measure again afterwards.
4. If **after 15 minutes** there is no improvement, give sugar again.
5. Once they recover, have them eat a light **snack** (bread, biscuits, fruit).
6. If they have **prescribed glucagon** (injectable or nasal), they can give it themselves or a trained person can, according to its instructions.
7. In **children** who will not cooperate with swallowing: consider putting **half a teaspoon of sugar** (about 2.5 g) **under the tongue**.
8. Call **112** if they are or become **unconscious**, if they have a seizure or if they do not improve.

## Warnings
- **Never** give anything by mouth to an unconscious person: they may choke. If they are breathing, recovery position and call 112.
