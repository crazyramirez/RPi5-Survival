> Guidance summary. Treat hypothermia first if present: it is more dangerous.

## Signs
White, greyish or yellowish skin that is cold, hard or waxy and **numb**, especially on the fingers, toes, nose, ears and cheeks. When rewarmed it may hurt, swell and blister.

## Steps
1. Shelter the person from the cold and wind and **treat hypothermia first** ("Hypothermia" protocol).
2. Remove rings, watches and any wet or tight clothing or footwear.
3. **Do not rewarm if there is a risk of it freezing again**: refreezing causes more damage than waiting.
4. If you can prevent it from freezing again, **immerse** the area in water at **37–39 °C** (lukewarm, never hot) until the skin softens and regains its colour; this usually takes about 30 minutes.
5. If there is no warm water, let it thaw with body heat (for example, in another person's armpit).
6. Dry gently, separate the fingers or toes with gauze and cover with **clean, loose dressings**. Do not burst the blisters.
7. Raise the area and seek **medical care** as soon as possible. Call **112** if there are signs of hypothermia, if the affected area is large or if you cannot reach a health centre by your own means.

## Warnings
- **Do not** rub the area (neither with snow nor with your hands): it damages the tissues even more.
- **Do not** use direct heat (fire, stove, hairdryer, very hot bag): the numb area will burn.
- Avoid walking on feet that have already thawed unless it is essential to get to safety.
