> Guidance summary. Pharmacy ORS (oral rehydration salts) are preferable: use the homemade recipe only if there is no other option.

## Signs of severity (call 112 or go to the emergency department)
- Drowsiness, confusion or difficulty waking them.
- Has not passed urine for many hours, very sunken eyes, very dry mouth, cannot drink or vomits everything.
- Blood in the stools, high fever, or a baby crying without tears and with few wet nappies.

## Homemade oral rehydration solution (WHO recipe)
1. **1 litre of drinking water** (boiled and cooled, or treated: "Making water safe to drink" protocol).
2. **6 level teaspoons of sugar**.
3. **Half a level teaspoon of salt**.
4. Stir until dissolved. **It should taste no saltier than tears.**

## How to give it
1. Give **small, frequent sips** (with a spoon for young children). If they vomit, wait 10 minutes and continue more slowly.
2. After each loose stool, as WHO guidance: under 2 years, **50–100 ml**; from 2 to 10 years, **100–200 ml**; older, as much as they want to drink.
3. Continue **breastfeeding** and normal food as soon as they can tolerate it.
4. Make fresh solution every day and throw away any left over after 24 hours.

## Warnings
- **Do not** add more salt or more sugar: a badly prepared solution can make dehydration worse.
- Soft drinks, juices and sports drinks do not replace oral rehydration solution in dehydration caused by diarrhoea.
