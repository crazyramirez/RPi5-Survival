> Guidance summary. Carbon monoxide cannot be seen or smelled: suspect it if several people in the same place feel unwell at the same time.

## Signs and sources
Headache, dizziness, nausea, tiredness or confusion, which improve on going outside and return on going back in. Common sources: **braziers**, gas or butane heaters, poorly ventilated boilers and water heaters, **generators**, barbecues or engines in enclosed spaces.

## Steps
1. **Get out into the fresh air** immediately and bring the others out if you can do so without risk. Open doors and windows on your way out.
2. If it is on your way, switch off the suspect appliance; do not linger.
3. Call **112** from outside.
4. **Do not go back in** until the emergency services allow it.
5. If someone does not respond: if they are breathing, **recovery position**; if they are not breathing normally, **CPR**.
6. Everyone exposed must be assessed by a health professional, especially **pregnant women, children, older people** and anyone who lost consciousness, even if they already feel better.

## Warnings
- Do not go into a place full of smoke or gas to rescue anyone without protection: alert the fire service.
- **Never** use generators, barbecues, braziers or outdoor heaters inside the home, the garage or a tent.
- Install a **carbon monoxide detector** and have boilers, water heaters and chimneys checked.
