> Summary for guidance only. After any sting, watch for signs of a severe allergic reaction (anaphylaxis).

## Bee or wasp
1. If the **sting** is left in (bee), remove it **as soon as possible** by scraping sideways with a fingernail or the edge of a card. Avoid pinching it.
2. Wash the area with soap and water.
3. Apply **cold** wrapped in a cloth and raise the area.
4. Call **112** if there is difficulty breathing, swelling of the lips, tongue or throat, dizziness or hives all over the body: this is **anaphylaxis** (use their auto-injector if they have one). Also if there are **many stings** or the sting is **in the mouth or throat**.

## Tick
1. Remove it **as soon as possible** with **fine-tipped tweezers**.
2. Grip it **as close to the skin as possible** and pull upwards with **steady** force, without twisting or jerking.
3. If part of the mouth remains and does not come out easily, leave it and let the skin heal.
4. Wash the area and your hands with soap and water and disinfect the skin.
5. Write down the date. See a health professional if in the **following weeks** a **fever**, malaise or a spreading red patch appears.

## Warnings
- **Do not** put oil, alcohol, petroleum jelly, nail polish or heat on the tick, and do not crush it with your fingers.
- Check the whole body (children and pets too) after walking in the countryside.
