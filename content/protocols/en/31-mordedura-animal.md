> Summary for guidance only. Every mammal bite should be assessed by a health professional because of the risk of infection, tetanus and, in some cases, rabies.

## Steps
1. Get to safety away from the animal; do not try to capture it if it is dangerous.
2. If it is bleeding heavily, **press** with a clean dressing, call **112** and follow the "Severe bleeding" protocol.
3. **Wash the wound immediately** with **soap and running water** for **at least 15 minutes**, even if it is a scratch.
4. If you have one, apply an **antiseptic** (for example, povidone-iodine) and cover with a clean dressing.
5. Go to a **health centre** the same day: they will assess antibiotics, a **tetanus vaccine** and, if appropriate, **rabies prophylaxis**.
6. Write down which animal it was, whether it has an owner and whether it is vaccinated: the health authorities may need to **observe the animal**.
7. Seek help **urgently** after any bite, scratch or contact with a **bat** (in Spain they can transmit the rabies virus), with a wild animal or one behaving strangely, or with an animal abroad, in Ceuta or Melilla.

## Warnings
- **Do not close** the wound yourself or cover it with home remedies.
- Do not touch a bat or a suspect animal, alive or dead, with bare hands; notify 112.
- Watch for signs of infection (spreading redness, pus, fever) over the following days.
