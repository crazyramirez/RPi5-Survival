> Summary for guidance only. In a prolonged power cut the greatest risks are fire (candles), carbon monoxide (generators, braziers) and spoiled food.

## Steps
1. Use battery-powered **torches** instead of candles. If you use candles, never leave them unattended.
2. **Unplug** sensitive appliances (computer, television) to protect them when the power comes back; leave one lamp switched on so you know when it returns.
3. Get information from a battery-powered or wind-up **radio**. Call **112** only for real emergencies.
4. Save your **mobile phone battery**: power-saving mode, low brightness and data turned off except for what is essential.
5. Keep **the fridge and freezer closed**: the fridge keeps cold for about **4 hours**; a full freezer, about **48 hours** (24 if it is half full).
6. Keep a reserve of **water**: pumping may fail, especially on upper floors.
7. Check on **elderly** or dependent **neighbours** and those who use electrical medical equipment.
8. Avoid lifts and drive with caution: traffic lights may not be working.

## Warnings
- **Never** use generators, barbecues, camping stoves or braziers indoors: risk of **carbon monoxide**.
- Throw away perishable food that has been above 4 °C for more than 2 hours.
- If you depend on electrical medical equipment, have a back-up plan and tell your health centre about it.
