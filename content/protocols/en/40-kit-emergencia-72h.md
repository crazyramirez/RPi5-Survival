> Summary for guidance only. The European Union recommends being able to fend for yourself for at least **72 hours** after an emergency. Check the kit from time to time (expiry dates, batteries, medication).

## Water and food
- **Water**: about **3 litres per person per day** for drinking and cooking (minimum 1.5 litres for drinking alone). For 3 days, about 9 litres per person.
- **Non-perishable food** that does not need cooking: tinned food (with a manual tin opener), nuts, bars, biscuits.
- Food for babies and pets if there are any.

## Light, power and communication
- Battery-powered or wind-up **radio**, **torch** (preferably a head torch) and spare batteries.
- A charged **power bank** and phone cable.
- A **whistle** to call for help.

## Health and hygiene
- First aid **kit** and **regular medication** for several days, with copies of the prescriptions.
- Hand sanitiser gel, wipes, rubbish bags, toilet paper and face masks.
- Water purification tablets or bleach suitable for drinking water.

## Documents and other items
- **Copies of documents** (ID card, health card, insurance) in a waterproof bag.
- **Cash** in small notes: card terminals and cash machines may not work.
- Spare keys, lighter or matches, multi-purpose pocket knife, duct tape, emergency thermal blanket, warm clothing and comfortable footwear.
- A paper list with **contact phone numbers** and a **meeting point** agreed with the family.

## Warnings
- Keep the kit in a place where it is easy to grab on your way out and make sure everyone at home knows where it is.
- The kit does not replace calling for help: in any emergency with injured people or danger, call **112**.
