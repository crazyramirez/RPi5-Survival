> Summary for guidance only. If you can make a call, **112** is always the first option.

## Calling 112
1. Call **112**: it works throughout the EU and can use another mobile network if yours has no coverage.
2. Say **where you are** (GPS coordinates), what has happened and how many of you there are. Many mobile phones also send their location automatically when you call.
3. If coverage is poor, try from a higher point and save battery between calls.

## Signals without a phone
1. **Mountain distress signal**: **6 signals per minute** (whistle, torch or shouts, one every 10 seconds), a one-minute pause and repeat. The rescuers' reply is 3 signals per minute.
2. **SOS** with light or sound: **3 short, 3 long, 3 short**.
3. **Towards a helicopter or aircraft**: standing, with **both arms raised in a Y shape** means "I need help"; one arm up and the other down, "I do not need help".
4. **Ground signals**, large and high-contrast (stones, branches, clothing): **V** = I need help; **X** = I need medical assistance.
5. By day, reflect the sun towards the aircraft with a **mirror** or a shiny surface. At night, use the torch.

## Warnings
- **Stay in a visible place** and, if you are lost, do not keep walking aimlessly: it makes it easier for them to find you.
- Wear brightly coloured clothing or spread something eye-catching on the ground.
- Do not light a fire to signal if there is a risk of wildfire.
